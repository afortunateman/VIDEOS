# Working with this user

Learned over the whole Staircase project. Read at the start of a project.

## What they want
- Ambition: "smooth", "beautiful", "award-winning", "polished", "studio quality",
  "first impression best impression". Go all out on craft, but check before long work with
  a test frame.
- Visual storytelling over narration. Every scene must make sense with the sound off.
- Emotion first: faces visible, expressions matching the line, breathing room for music.
- They think in cinematographer terms (panning shot, close-up, top view, zoom out) and
  describe fixes by what they feel ("feels like two diff scenes", "looks 2D", "unreal").
  Translate the feeling into the technical cause (camera restart, flat light, UV stretch) and
  fix that.

## How they give notes
- Short, fast, often with typos; numbered lists of changes; screenshots of the problem frame.
- "Don't change anything unnecessary", "leave everything else untouched", "just give the last
  part", "dont redo the whole thing": patch only the named shots and send just that part.
- "Be careful" / "don't waste too much compute": render stills to confirm before video.
- When they say "no" to an idea (new title music), drop it completely; don't half-keep it.
- They ask "status?" during long renders: answer with done / remaining / ETA in one or two
  lines, and send what's finished.
- "Just finish up and give it to me": stop iterating, deliver the best current version.

## Deliverables they like
- Send the file itself (share copy) plus say where the master is.
- Explain differences simply (viewing copy vs master).
- Credits as "Created by Varun Kamalahasan"; dedication "For Mom, Dad and Tofu".
- Fonts: rounded chunky (Fredoka); title lettering orange fill with dark maroon outline;
  credits white on black.
